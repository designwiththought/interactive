Layout

Pick a spacing scale and never freehand a gap again

Tip 03 · 3 min read · updated June 2026

Most layouts that feel “a little off” aren’t off in some mysterious way — they’re spaced with random numbers. A 13 here, a 22 there, a 7 because it looked right at 2am. A spacing scale takes that decision off your plate.

The scale

Pick a small set of values built on a base of 4 and use only those:

4
8
12
16
24
32
48

Why 4? It divides cleanly, it scales to common type sizes, and the steps are far enough apart that the difference between any two is visible. Eight versus twelve reads as a real change; eight versus nine is just noise.

Put it to work

  1. Add the values as number variables — space/1 = 4 through space/8 = 48 — the same way you’d store your radii.
  2. Bind gap and padding in your auto-layout frames to those tokens instead of typing numbers.
  3. When something feels cramped, jump to the next step up — don’t invent a value in between.
The discipline is the point. The scale only works if you refuse the in-between numbers. “Close enough” values are exactly what make a layout feel sloppy.

What you get back

Spacing decisions go from “what number?” to “which step?” — a much smaller question. Your work picks up a quiet consistency that reads as polish, and any two designers on the file reach for the same gaps without a meeting about it.
