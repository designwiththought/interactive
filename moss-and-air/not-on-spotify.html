I. Note

moss & air isn't on Spotify.

There are two reasons, and they're related. One is about money, one is about machines.

If you came looking for the music, scroll to III. Where to listen — it's on Bandcamp, where it belongs.

II. Why

on the money, and the machines.

Spotify pays artists somewhere around three-tenths of a cent per stream. A track played a million times earns roughly three thousand dollars before distribution fees, splits, and currency conversion. For an independent record made slowly in a cabin and pressed in small editions, that arithmetic doesn't work — and it isn't supposed to. The platform's economics are built around scale, not around the people who make the records.

Bandcamp pays artists between 82% and 100% of every sale. Apple Music and Tidal pay roughly double Spotify's per-stream rate. SoundCloud shares revenue more directly with the people uploading the music. None of these are perfect, but the gap is large enough to matter.

The second reason is what Spotify has chosen to become. Over the past two years it has rolled out an AI DJ, AI-curated playlists, and a steady tolerance for fully machine-generated tracks competing for the same royalty pool as work made by people. The platform was built on the back of human musicians and is now systematically replacing them with cheaper, faster substitutes. I don't want this project sitting inside that.
